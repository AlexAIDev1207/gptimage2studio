## Start With a Character Lock, Not a Scene Prompt

The biggest mistake in character work is starting with scene language instead of identity language.

People often write prompts like this:

`Create a stylish young woman in a cafe, cinematic lighting, modern outfit, realistic skin, laptop, coffee, city background.`

That prompt may produce a nice image, but it does not create GPT Image 2 consistent characters. It creates a scene with a person inside it. The model has too much freedom to change face structure, styling, proportions, and visual anchors every time you rerun it.

Start with a character lock instead.

Your character lock should define:

1. age range
2. face shape
3. hair style and hair length
4. skin tone
5. body build
6. signature accessory
7. signature prop
8. default wardrobe cues

For example:

`Young woman, sharp dark bob haircut, oval face, olive skin, medium height, slim build, silver hoop earrings, olive trench coat, red notebook as recurring prop.`

That is already much closer to a system. GPT Image 2 consistent characters works better when identity details are stable and scene details are variable.

## Build a Reusable Character Sheet and Reference Pack

The first successful output should not be scene one. It should be the master reference.

That is how serious continuity workflows work. Before you ask for narrative scenes, you build the person.

Your master character sheet should include:

- front view
- side view
- three-quarter view
- full-body neutral pose
- close-up portrait
- smiling expression
- serious expression
- one clear prop view

This gives GPT Image 2 consistent characters a stronger anchor than a single fashionable portrait ever could.

In practice, the best reference pack usually contains three image types:

1. `Face anchor`
   - close portrait
   - good lighting
   - stable hairstyle
2. `Full-body anchor`
   - proportions
   - posture
   - wardrobe silhouette
3. `Prop or accessory anchor`
   - notebook, earrings, glasses, hat, branded object, or other recurring marker

GPT Image 2 consistent characters gets easier when at least one signature anchor stays visible even as the scene changes. That is why recurring earrings, jackets, or props matter so much.

## Prompt Templates for Same Character, New Scene

You do not need fifty prompts. You need three reusable structures.

### 1. Master character prompt

Use this first:

`Create a clean character reference sheet of the same woman: sharp dark bob haircut, oval face, olive skin, silver hoop earrings, olive trench coat, slim build, medium height, recurring red notebook. Include front view, side view, three-quarter view, full-body neutral pose, and two facial expressions. Keep facial identity perfectly consistent. Clean studio background, realistic anatomy, controlled neutral light.`

This is a true GPT Image 2 consistent characters prompt because it asks for repeatability before storytelling.

### 2. Same character, new scene prompt

Use this after the master sheet works:

`Use the same character identity as the master reference: sharp dark bob haircut, oval face, olive skin, silver hoop earrings, red notebook, same body proportions. Place her in a modern cafe at morning light, eye-level medium shot, realistic atmosphere, keep facial identity and wardrobe silhouette consistent.`

### 3. Two-character continuity prompt

This is useful for dialogue scenes or campaigns:

`Create two recurring characters with fixed identities across all panels. Character one keeps the sharp dark bob haircut, silver hoop earrings, olive trench coat, and red notebook. Character two keeps short curly hair, navy overshirt, warm brown skin, and black square glasses. Maintain face structure, height difference, and recurring wardrobe cues consistently in every frame.`

The point is simple: GPT Image 2 consistent characters depends on identity instructions that survive scene variation.

## Keep the Same Character Across Angles, Outfits, and Lighting

Most continuity failures happen because users change too many variables at once.

The stable order is:

1. angle
2. outfit
3. lighting

If you jump straight to “new outfit, new city, night rain, action pose, wide shot, dramatic neon,” GPT Image 2 consistent characters becomes much harder.

### Same scene, four camera angles

First prove continuity under controlled framing changes. Keep the location fixed. Change only the camera.

This is why `same character, different angles` is a better test than `same character, different worlds`.

### New outfit, same face

After angles hold, change clothing while preserving:

- face structure
- hairstyle
- body proportions
- signature accessories
- recurring prop

### Different lighting without identity drift

Only after that should you move into daylight, dusk, studio, neon, or interior shadow variants. Many people blame GPT Image 2 consistent characters when a person looks different in a new lighting setup. Sometimes the issue is not identity drift. It is that poor lighting changed perceived face shape or skin texture.

## Fix Character Drift With Edits and Targeted Repairs

Even a good continuity workflow drifts sometimes. That is normal.

The real skill is knowing when to regenerate and when to repair.

Regenerate when:

- the face structure is fundamentally different
- the body proportions changed
- the hairstyle is clearly off
- the scene composition is weak anyway

Edit when:

- one eye shape drifted slightly
- the earrings disappeared
- the notebook changed shape
- the jacket detail shifted
- the lighting changed the character more than intended

Useful repair language:

- keep the same face shape and hairstyle as the reference
- keep the same silver hoop earrings
- keep the red notebook unchanged
- keep body proportions unchanged
- only adjust the jacket color and sleeve shape

This is where GPT Image 2 consistent characters becomes more efficient than random rerolling. You are no longer generating “a similar person.” You are preserving a defined identity.

If your next goal is motion, stop after continuity is solid. Do not over-edit the stills into instability. Move them into the [Seedance 2 workflow](/blog/gpt-image-2-seedance-2-workflow) while the asset pack is still clean.

## QA Checklist, Limits, and Final Advice

GPT Image 2 consistent characters is powerful, but it is not magic. There are still failure zones.

Check these before you approve a sequence:

1. face shape
2. eye spacing
3. hairline
4. height and body ratio
5. recurring accessory
6. recurring prop
7. wardrobe silhouette
8. lighting consistency across the sequence

Be honest about the limits too.

GPT Image 2 consistent characters gets harder when:

- scenes are crowded
- lighting becomes extreme
- too many edits pile onto the same base image
- the identity was never clearly defined in the first place
